# Redesigning a site that already exists

The commonest way a redesign goes wrong is treating it as a blank page. Decide the mode first.

## Name the mode

- **Keep the brand.** The site should look current and stay recognisable. Work from what is there.
- **A new look.** New visual language over the same content and structure.
- **A blank page.** No site worth keeping, or the brand itself is changing. Then this is ordinary
  new work.

If it is unclear which and the person is there, ask once (otherwise keep the brand, and say so): "Should it stay recognisably the same brand, or are we starting
the look again?"

## Look before touching

Write down what is there before proposing anything:

- the brand's colours, typefaces, logo treatment and corner radius;
- the page tree, the main navigation, the paths that lead to a sale or a signup;
- which blocks of content do real work and which are filler;
- what is recognisably theirs: a gesture, a hero, a tone of voice;
- what should go: habits from `page/habits.md`, broken layouts, dead links, stock pictures;
- where the three dials sit today. That reading, not the usual starting values, is where you begin;
- what search already sends people to: titles, descriptions, structured data. Losing that is the
  largest risk a redesign carries.

## What stays unless the owner says otherwise

- Addresses of pages and anchors, and the labels in the main navigation.
- The order and names of form fields.
- The logo and wordmark.
- Legal, consent and cookie text.
- The brand's colour: a brand that is purple stays purple.
- The voice of the copy. A new look is not a rewrite.
- Whatever already works for keyboard and screen-reader users.
- The names analytics depends on: buttons, fields, section ids.

## Changes, from least risk to most

Stop as soon as the brief is met.

1. Type: the largest visible lift for the least risk.
2. Spacing and vertical rhythm.
3. Colour: fewer, calmer neutrals around the same brand colour.
4. Motion on components that already exist.
5. A recomposed hero and key sections.
6. Replacing a block outright, only when it cannot be saved.

When the structure, the content and the search standing are sound, the first four usually give
most of the value. A full redesign is for a site whose structure is what is broken.
