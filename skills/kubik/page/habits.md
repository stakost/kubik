# Habits of machine-made pages

What a page looks like when it was assembled rather than designed. Know these. Using one is fine
when you chose it and can say why; sliding into one is what this list is for. Read it when a page
starts to feel like every other page.

## At the top

- A version tag or "beta" badge above the headline, when the page is not about a launch.
- A strip of small capitals along the bottom of the hero listing three nouns.
- A line of fine print under the buttons saying what it works with.
- A cue telling the reader to scroll.
- A place name, a local time or the weather, for a product that is not about a place.

## Labels and numbering

- A small uppercase label over every single section.
- Sections numbered "01", "02", "03" when their order carries no meaning.
- "1 / 4" on tiles and pictures.
- Steps called "Step 1", "Stage 2", "Phase 03" instead of what the step is.
- A coloured dot in front of every item, when the dot reports no real status.
- A row of facts separated by middle dots.

## Composition

- A section heading on the left with a small paragraph floating at the top right, aligned to
  nothing.
- Hairlines above and below every row of a long list.
- One block of the page set on its own left edge, a few pixels off the column the rest keep. The column has one left edge and every block starts on it; a wider block is wider on both sides.
- Lines and crosshairs drawn only to look technical.
- Text turned ninety degrees along an edge, for no reason the content gives.
- Bars with a filled track behind them, for a comparison of two numbers.

## Pictures

- A fake product window built from grey boxes, passed off as a screenshot.
- A tag or caption laid over a photograph.
- A mock photo credit under a stock picture.
- A build number or "synced 4s ago" inside a mock interface.

## Words

- A headline broken across lines with one word in italics, as the only idea.
- "Quietly trusted by", "field notes", "from the workshop": borrowed modesty.
- A sentence under a heading that comments on the section instead of saying something.
- Precise-looking figures nobody measured.
- The same thought in the hero, in a band and again in the cards.

## The dash

In English copy you write for a page, do not lean on the long dash. It is the most recognisable
tic of generated prose. Use two sentences, a comma, a colon or parentheses. Three things this does
not reach: text the user or the brand supplied, which stays as written; other languages, which
keep their own typography (in Russian the dash is ordinary punctuation); and ranges of numbers,
which take a short dash or a hyphen, one of them consistently.
