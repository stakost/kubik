# Working from a reference

"Like this site." "Something in the spirit of that product." A screenshot, a link, a name, a
design record from somewhere. A reference is the clearest wish a person can give, and the easiest
one to get wrong in two ways: by ignoring it, and by copying it.

## Look, then name what makes it itself

Look at the reference with whatever you have (a browser, a screenshot, the record). If you cannot
look, say so, and work from what is commonly known about it, naming that as the source.

Then write down, in your own words:

- **its line**: the whole look in a phrase ("a typeset terminal on white paper", "a gallery wall
  with one loud picture");
- **its five decisions**, the ones that would make it recognisable with the logo covered:
  - the ground and the light: paper, dark, tinted; flat or lit;
  - the voice of the type: what family of face, how large the largest, how tight;
  - the discipline of the accent: how many colours, and what the accent is allowed to touch;
  - shape and depth: the corners, and how one thing sits above another;
  - rhythm: how dense, how much air, how sections follow each other;
  - pictures, when they matter more than any of the above: how large, how cropped, how much
    space around them.

Most looks are carried by two or three of these. Find which.

## Take the decisions, not the pixels

| Take | Leave |
|---|---|
| the discipline (one accent, no shadows, huge type) | the exact accent colour of someone else's brand |
| the rhythm and the density | their layout, section for section |
| the kind of typeface | their licensed or custom face by name, unless the person owns it |
| one device that fits the subject | their illustrations, icons, photographs |
| the structure of how a page argues | their words |

Far enough that nobody would take the result for theirs; near enough that the person sees what
they asked for. If you cannot say what you changed, you copied.

## Whose reference is it

- **The person's own brand or an earlier piece of theirs**: its cards are settled. Continue it
  exactly; this is `system.md` territory, and a `DESIGN.md` in the project is read first.
- **Someone else's product**: it leans the dice. Its decisions become weights on the matching
  cards, and the throw still happens for the rest.
- **Several references**: take one decision from each, and say which from which. Three looks
  averaged together are no look.

## Say what you took

The closing note carries one line per reference: its line, the decisions taken from it, and what
was deliberately changed.
