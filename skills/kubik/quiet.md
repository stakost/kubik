# Quiet: flat, simple, and still somebody's work

Any kind can be asked for quiet: a page, a report, a deck, a page of figures. Clean, flat, simple,
strict. It is the hardest register, because nothing is left to hide behind. A quiet piece and a
plain one have the same number of gradients, which is none; the difference is that in the quiet
one every remaining thing was decided.

Read this after the kind file (or the style file, when a page has one), when the register is
quiet. It changes how you use the shelves, not which file you follow.

## What goes

- Gradients, glows, shadows, blur, glass, grain, texture.
- Anything that loops. Anything that moves without being asked.
- A second accent colour. A third typeface.
- Decoration that stands for an idea the page does not have.

## What stays, and carries everything

- **The method.** Still a subject, still three concepts, still one signature moment. A quiet
  piece has a concept as much as a loud one; it just says it in fewer marks.
- **Scale.** The largest type is at least four times the body size, except in a system for
  software people work in, where the largest type is a page title and stays modest
  (`system.md`). Without depth, contrast of size is where the drama lives.
- **One typeface with character**, in two or three sizes and two weights, set with care: balanced
  headline lines, tabular figures, real quotation marks, punctuation that hangs. A mono for data
  beside it is the second face, and the last.
- **A grid you can feel**, with margins wider than seems necessary, and a spacing scale that
  repeats (the same few distances everywhere).
- **One accent, used a handful of times**, so that each use means something: the link, the figure
  that matters, the one mark. It keeps that one meaning: the focus ring and the hover state take
  ink, not the accent, when the accent already stands for something.
- **One flat ground.** Quiet is not a colour: an ink-dark ground is as quiet as paper when it is a
  single tone.
- **Quiet is not pale.** Few colours means each one is deep and sure: ink that is really dark, an
  accent that is really that colour, a ground that is white, paper or dark rather than a wash of
  the accent. A quiet piece reads as faded when its tints are timid and its type is medium: then
  it is a form from an office, not a design. `method.md` "Of its time" says what keeps it current.
- **One drawn thing, made well**: a line, a diagram, a mark, a rule that does something. Flat, in
  the accent or in ink.
- **Hairlines and alignment** instead of boxes and cards.
- **Response.** A quiet piece still answers the touch: a rule thickens, a row tints, a button
  presses. Response is not motion and is never what gets cut.

## Leaning the dice

Throw the kind's dice as usual, with three changes:

- take out the cards that are made of depth, texture or looping motion (glass, grain, aurora,
  marquee, floating object) before you roll;
- a device card you are dealt is taken in its flat form: a path that is drawn, not one that draws
  itself on a loop; a tile grid without elevation;
- weight the worlds that are quiet by nature, and keep the others in the roll at weight one, so a
  quiet piece can still surprise:

| Kind | Worlds that are quiet by nature |
|---|---|
| a page | `styles/minimal.md` when the person said "minimal" or "editorial"; otherwise `page.md` with its dials on the calm row of its table |
| a report | the Swiss memo, the field report, the broadsheet |
| slides | the Swiss poster, the museum label, keynote black, the whiteboard |
| figures | the consulting page, the investor letter, the annual report |

## Reading the audit

FRAME is read as always. RICHNESS is read the other way round:

- gradients, shadows, blurs, endless animations: at or near zero, and that is the intent;
- largest type: high. If it is under four times the body size, the piece is plain, not quiet
  (a system is the exception, above);
- the response line: not zero. Every control answers;
- one SVG or drawn detail of real size: present.

Then the test no script can run. Cover the content with your hand and ask what is left that
somebody chose: a proportion, a margin, a typeface, a line. If the answer is nothing, it is a
default. A stranger shown the piece for five seconds should be able to say the one thing it did.
